About me

Full CV →

I’m a wireless systems engineer at Apple, working on RF front-end systems for cellular connectivity.

For my PhD at Virginia Tech, advised by Prof. Jeffrey Reed, Prof. Michael Buehrer and Prof. Harpreet Dhillon, I developed theory and methods for wireless localization when the direct radio path is blocked. The work brings together electromagnetic theory, to model how signals diffract around building edges; estimation theory, to establish fundamental limits on localization accuracy; and optimization, to design efficient algorithms and optimal anchor placements. It was funded by a U.S. Department of Justice NIJ Graduate Research Fellowship ($166,500) and won first place in Virginia Tech’s Torgersen Graduate Student Research Excellence Award.

For my M.Tech. at IIIT Delhi with Prof. Shobha Sundar Ram, I applied signal processing to design a Doppler-resilient IEEE 802.11ad waveform and an automotive radar built on it.

Outside work, I enjoy riding bikes. You can find my rides on Strava and YouTube.

Diffraction-Aided Wireless Positioning

Gaurav Duggal, R. Michael Buehrer, Harpreet S. Dhillon, Jeffrey H. Reed

IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, 2025

Revisits electromagnetic diffraction theory to model how signals bend around building edges when the direct path is blocked. Estimating these bent path lengths directly greatly improves 3D and floor-level (z-axis) accuracy when locating people inside buildings.

Paper

Optimal Anchor Placement for Wireless Localization in Mixed LOS and NLOS Scenarios

Gaurav Duggal, R. Michael Buehrer, Harpreet S. Dhillon, Jeffrey H. Reed

IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, 2026 · Open access

A Fisher-information framework for placing localization anchors when some signal paths are blocked. It extends optimal placement from one known target to a whole region, giving two practical algorithms for robust designs across line-of-sight and blocked conditions.

Paper

Doppler-Resilient 802.11ad-Based Automotive Joint Radar-Communications

Gaurav Duggal, S. Vishwakarma, Kumar Vijay Mishra, Shobha Sundar Ram

IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems, 2020

Adds Prouhet-Thue-Morse sequences to the 802.11ad preamble so one signal serves as both a communication link and a radar, staying reliable at speeds up to ±144 km/h and revealing the fine motion of pedestrians, bicycles and cars.
